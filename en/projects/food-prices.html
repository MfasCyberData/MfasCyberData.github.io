01 / Overview

The goal was to turn a long, heterogeneous history into information that could be compared across time and geography.

The project uses more than 47,750 World Food Programme records spanning roughly twenty years. I structured the analysis around three questions: how do prices evolve over time, what differences appear across regions, and what historical patterns can support an exploratory forecast?

03 / Analysis

Visualizations answer questions; they are not decoration around the dataset.

The project combines descriptive analysis, regional views and seasonal reading. R and tidyverse support transformations and aggregations, while the visual outputs make trends and comparisons easier to interpret.

04 / Forecasting

Prophet is used as a forecasting baseline, not as a promise about the future.

The prepared series were used to produce six-month forecasts. The project's value sits as much in reproducible preparation and interpretation as in the model output itself.

Interpretation: a forecast is a scenario based on available history. It needs context and limitations, especially in markets exposed to external shocks.

05 / Limits

This demonstrates an Analytics workflow, not a production forecasting service.

Scope

Academic project

No production service, alerting or automated drift monitoring.

Model

Prophet as baseline

A practical baseline for the exercise, not a substitute for a broader comparison of forecasting approaches.
